Vitali Valtanen

PhD Student and Junior Researcher, Art & Design, Estonian Academy of Arts

linkedin, instagram

“My work is driven by a simple but urgent question: what happens to design when non-human animals are no longer objects of care or concern, but recognised as active agents in shaping the spaces we share? Working at the intersection of posthumanist design theory and critical animal studies, I investigate how urban environments, and the design decisions embedded in them, reproduce speciesist logics that exclude non-human life, while drawing on vegan theory as a political and methodological anchor. Through fieldwork and interspecies design tools I try to take animal agency seriously. Not as a metaphor, but as a design condition. For me, the posthumanist lens is inseparable from a political and ethical commitment: to dismantle the anthropocentric assumptions baked into design practice, and to cultivate frameworks where more-than-human coexistence is treated as basic rather than added on at the end.”

“I grew up surrounded by other species: experiment subjects in my mother’s biology lab, commodities in my economics textbooks, and beloved companions at home. That early confusion, loving what the world around me also consumed and instrumentalised, has never fully resolved itself, and I have stopped trying to resolve it. Instead, this confusion and my speculation of multispecies relationships has become the engine of my practice. In the past, I have lived with mycelium, co-designed with birds, and imagined cat hair as weapons: each project a way of staying with the discomfort of entanglement rather than designing past it. Working at the intersection of STS, design methodology, and human-robot interaction, I understand making not as human authorship but as a negotiation with agencies that resist, redirect, and surprise. ”

Ana Robles Pérez

Researcher and visual-documentary designer. Educator at the Fine Arts Department, Royal Academy of Arts The Hague nl (KABK). External co-founder of the Lab Post Extractivist Assemblies. 

personal website, Instagram

“A dog, a “dog justice” and a dog researcher. Posthumanist thought allows a ground to speculate on a form of “dog justice,” expanding the discourse around an animal we have positioned within a uniquely ambiguous condition. A figure between the control and the freedom, the governed and the autonomous, neither fully nature nor fully cultural, biologically rooted but socially shaped, dependent yet agent, still innocent, voiceless, apolitical. As a human-made figure whose place in the world remains difficult to locate, the dog exposes the constructed nature of species boundaries. Its existence renders visible the infrastructures—colonial, urban, and affective—that sustain life across species, while also revealing their fractures. In doing so, it prompts us to ask: What does the dog reveal about the ethical relationships we have forged with other beings, and with the environments we inhabit together?”
